Privacy Policy

Effective 27 October 2026 Applies to io.stashy.app Contact hellostashy@gmail.com

Stashy reads your bank and UPI notifications so you don't have to type transactions in by hand, and it does that entirely on your phone. This page explains exactly what the app looks at, where that information goes, and what never leaves your device.

01

Overview

Stashy is a personal expense tracker for Android. It automatically detects UPI and bank transaction alerts as they arrive, extracts the amount, merchant and type from the notification text, and organizes them into a spending dashboard: categorized, charted, budgeted and exportable.

There is no Stashy account, no sign-in, no analytics, no ad network and no tracking. Stashy shows a few clearly labelled ads of its own (§07); they are never chosen from your data, and Stashy never reports which ads you saw or tapped. Every transaction Stashy shows you was parsed and stored on the phone in your hand, and stays there unless you export it, or turn on the optional sync between your own devices described in §06. Stashy runs no server of its own: sync goes through a server you choose, and only in encrypted form that nobody but your own devices can read.

02

Information we access

Stashy accesses only what it needs to build your spending record:

  • Notification content from banking and payment apps: the text of alerts your bank or UPI app posts (e.g. "₹450 debited to Amazon"), read in order to detect and parse transactions. This can include the last few digits of your account number as shown in the alert, which Stashy uses to tell your accounts apart.
  • What you enter yourself: manual transactions, notes, categories, budgets, savings goals, names you give your accounts, and the balance you tell Stashy you have.
  • The camera, only while you scan a pairing code: when you set up sync on a second device and tap "Scan pairing code", the camera is used to read that QR code. Images are processed on the device, and are never stored or sent.

If you turn on App lock, Android's own fingerprint or screen-lock prompt is shown. Stashy is only told whether it succeeded; it never receives your fingerprint, face or PIN.

Stashy does not access your contacts, location, microphone, call log, or messages outside of notifications. It never asks for your bank login, card number, or UPI PIN. It has no way to move money and never sees your credentials.

03

How notification access works

Android's Notification Listener permission is broad by design: an app granted it can technically see every notification on the phone. Stashy narrows that down deliberately:

  • Incoming notifications are checked against a list of known bank and UPI sender packages and keywords before anything is parsed.
  • Notifications that don't match a recognized financial pattern are discarded immediately and never stored.
  • Matched notification text is parsed on-device using pattern rules built into the app. No cloud service, third-party API or AI model ever receives the raw notification.
Why this permission is requested

Notification access is what lets Stashy log a transaction the moment your bank confirms it, without you typing anything. It is requested for that single purpose. You can revoke it at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Special access → Notification access; Stashy simply stops auto-capturing until it's granted again.

04

How we use it

Your transactions and the things you enter are used only to:

  • Build your dashboard: totals, trends, the spending calendar, budgets, savings goals, upcoming repeating payments and per-account totals.
  • Group spending by category, and remember merchant → category choices you've made before, so repeat purchases file themselves.
  • Answer questions you ask the in-app assistant. The assistant runs on fixed rules inside the app, on your phone; your questions are not sent anywhere.
  • Show a summary on the home screen widget, if you add one. While App lock or Hide amounts is on, the widget shows no figures.
  • Generate the CSV, PDF or backup files you ask for.

None of this involves sending data anywhere. There is no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, no advertising network and no advertising ID in Stashy. The only thing Stashy downloads for its ads is one public file, and it sends nothing about you to get it (§07). If that ever changes, this page will be updated first, and the change will be reflected in the app's Data Safety section on Google Play before it ships.

05

Where your data lives

Everything Stashy records is kept in a local database on your device. Stashy has no backend and runs no server, so there is no copy of your data held by us, and no other user of the app can access it.

Not in Google's backup. From version 1.3.4, Stashy opts out of Android's cloud backup, so your phone's own backup to your Google account does not include your Stashy data. Reinstalling Stashy starts it fresh.

Moving to a new phone. If you set up a new phone with Android's phone-to-phone transfer (by cable or direct Wi-Fi), your Stashy data goes straight from the old phone to the new one. You can also move it with a Stashy backup file or with sync (§06). On Android 8, Android's transfer does not include Stashy data, so use a backup file or sync there.

Earlier versions of Stashy did not opt out, so a copy made by Android's backup before 1.3.4 may still be in your Google account. You can remove it from your Google account's device backups.

06

Sync between your devices

Sync is optional and off until you turn it on. It keeps the same data on more than one of your own phones or tablets.

  • Encrypted on your phone first. When you turn sync on, your phone creates a random key (AES-256-GCM). Before anything is sent, your data is encrypted with that key. The key is given to your other devices only through the pairing QR code or pairing code you show them yourself. It is never sent to the server.
  • The server only holds a sealed file. The server stores the encrypted file under a random mailbox ID. It cannot read your transactions, amounts, categories or anything else in it, and neither can we. It is not given your name, email address, phone number or any account.
  • What the server can see. Like any internet server, it sees the network (IP) address your device connects from, when it connects, and the size of the encrypted file.
  • Your choice of server. Stashy does not run a sync server. You point it at one you choose: a WebDAV drive or small server of your own, or one run by someone you trust. The encrypted file is stored there, under that server's control, not ours.
  • When it runs. Sync runs when you open or leave the app, and when you tap Sync now. It never runs while sync is off.
Keep your pairing code private

Anyone holding your pairing code can join your sync and read your data, just as your own devices do. Only show it to a device you own.

07

Ads

Stashy shows one ad on each of its Home, Activity and Charts pages, always labelled AD. Most of the time it is a tip for one of Stashy's own features, built into the app. When a sponsor has booked a slot, it can be the sponsor's banner instead.

  • How sponsor ads arrive. About once a day, while you use the app, Stashy downloads a single public file listing the current sponsor ads, from https://hellostashy-prog.github.io/stashy-ads/ads.json. Every phone downloads the same file. The request carries no ID, no cookie, and nothing about you or your money.
  • Never chosen from your data. Which ad you see depends only on the page and today's date. Stashy never uses your transactions, notifications, accounts or anything else you've entered to pick an ad.
  • Nothing reported back. Stashy does not count, log or send which ads you saw or tapped. There is no advertising ID, no ad network and no tracking in the app.
  • Signed by Stashy. The file is digitally signed, and the app ignores any file that wasn't signed by Stashy, so nobody else can put an ad in front of you through it.
  • What the host can see. The file is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service run by GitHub, Inc. Like any website, GitHub sees the IP address your phone connects from and when it connects; GitHub's own privacy statement covers what it does with that. Stashy never receives those details.
  • Tapping an ad. A sponsor's ad opens the sponsor's website in your browser. Stashy adds nothing about you to the link. From there, the sponsor's own privacy policy applies. Tapping one of Stashy's own tips just opens that part of the app.
Offline

With no connection, Stashy shows its own built-in tips. Nothing about ads ever stops the app from working.

08

Sharing

Stashy does not sell, rent, or share your transaction data with any third party, including advertisers. Data leaves the app only when you choose to:

  • Export a CSV, PDF or backup file, which is saved to your device's Downloads folder;
  • Share an exported file through Android's standard share sheet to an app of your choosing; or
  • Turn on sync, which sends your data only in encrypted form (§06).

Exports and shares happen only when you tap them. Sync runs in the background only after you have turned it on. Downloading the ads file (§07) sends no data about you.

09

Retention & deletion

You can delete individual transactions, categories, budgets and goals at any time from within the app. Deleting something removes it from the local database, and, if sync is on, from your other devices at the next sync.

Uninstalling the app deletes everything it has stored on that device, including the downloaded ads file. If you used sync, the encrypted copy stays on the server you chose until it is removed there. It cannot be opened by anyone without your key.
